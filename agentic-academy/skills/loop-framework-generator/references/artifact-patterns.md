# Artifact Patterns

Use these defaults when the user is unsure what passes, checks, or human gates to use. Adapt them to the specific artifact.

## Newsletter Draft

Pass order:

1. Structure: every section has one job.
2. Redundancy: no two sections repeat the same point.
3. Argument: every section moves the core idea forward.
4. Evidence: claims have examples, caveats, sources, or softer wording.
5. Voice: remove language that sounds too polished, too certain, or too generic.
6. Rules: remove banned punctuation, banned phrases, unsupported metrics, and style-rule violations.
7. Finish: produce a final change log and list anything that still needs human judgment.

Checker:

- The draft keeps the original argument.
- No two sections do the same job.
- No repeated claim appears in adjacent sections.
- Every major claim has an example, caveat, source, or softer wording.
- The draft follows the style rules.
- The final output includes a change log.

Human gate:

- The fix would change the core argument.
- Two possible edits are both valid but lead to different meanings.
- A claim needs evidence the current files do not provide.
- The draft needs a new example, story, or opinion from the author.

## Landing Page

Pass order:

1. Offer clarity: the first screen explains what this is, who it is for, and why it matters.
2. CTA consistency: the page has one primary CTA and consistent button language.
3. Section completeness: required sections are present and no section is doing the same job twice.
4. Proof and caveats: claims have proof, context, caveats, or softer wording.
5. Mobile readability: important text and buttons are readable and usable on mobile.
6. Links and placeholders: links work, placeholders are removed, and unfinished copy is flagged.
7. Final QA: produce a final change log and list anything that still needs human judgment.

Checker:

- The first screen clearly explains the offer.
- The page has one primary CTA.
- CTA wording is consistent across the page.
- No placeholders remain.
- Every major claim has proof, context, caveat, or softer wording.
- Required sections are present.
- Links work, or broken links are listed.
- Mobile readability has been checked, or marked unresolved if the page cannot be inspected.

Human gate:

- The fix would change the offer promise.
- A claim needs proof the current files do not provide.
- Pricing, guarantee, deadline, or availability is unclear.
- Two CTA directions are both plausible but lead to different reader expectations.
- Visual or mobile QA cannot be completed from the available files.

## Brief Or Spec

Pass order:

1. Ambiguity: find places a reader could interpret the brief two different ways.
2. Missing decisions: list choices the owner still needs to make.
3. Evidence: mark claims as sourced, assumption, unsupported, or needs example.
4. Acceptance criteria: turn vague requirements into checkable conditions.
5. Next action: make sure the next person knows what to do first.
6. Caveats and risks: name limits, risks, dependencies, and open questions.
7. Final handoff: produce the cleaned brief plus unresolved decisions.

Checker:

- No important section can be read two ways without an open question.
- Every major claim is sourced, marked as an assumption, softened, or flagged.
- Every requirement has acceptance criteria or is marked as unresolved.
- Missing decisions are listed instead of silently filled in.
- The next action is clear.
- Risks, caveats, and dependencies are named.
- The final output includes a change log and unresolved decision list.

Human gate:

- A decision is missing and cannot be inferred from the source material.
- Two interpretations are both plausible.
- A claim needs evidence the current files do not provide.
- A requirement depends on priority, budget, timing, or audience.
- The brief needs a new example, constraint, or tradeoff from the owner.

## Research Evidence Loop

Pass order:

1. Source inventory: list provided sources and identify missing source types.
2. Claim extraction: pull major claims from the artifact.
3. Evidence matching: match each claim to a source or mark it unsupported.
4. Caveat pass: add limitations, uncertainty, dates, or scope boundaries.
5. Contradiction pass: flag claims that conflict with source material.
6. Citation pass: add source references or source notes where needed.
7. Final evidence report: list supported, softened, unresolved, and removed claims.

Checker:

- Every major claim is supported, softened, removed, or marked unresolved.
- Source dates are noted when freshness matters.
- Contradictions are flagged instead of smoothed over.
- Missing sources are listed.
- The final output includes an evidence report.

Human gate:

- The claim needs a source not currently available.
- Current facts may have changed and require live verification.
- The user must decide whether to remove or keep a weak claim.

## SEO Draft Readiness

Pass order:

1. Search intent: verify the draft answers the intended searcher question.
2. Title and description: improve clarity without clickbait.
3. Structure: headings match reader questions.
4. Internal links: suggest useful internal links if available.
5. Evidence and freshness: flag claims that need current verification.
6. Snippet pass: add concise definitions, lists, or summaries where useful.
7. Final SEO report: list changes and unresolved research needs.

Checker:

- The target keyword or topic is clear.
- The draft answers the likely search intent.
- Headings are descriptive.
- Claims that depend on current facts are flagged.
- Suggested metadata matches the actual post promise.

Human gate:

- Current search intent needs live research.
- The title choice changes the promise of the post.
- The draft needs evidence or examples not present in the source material.

## SOP Or Process Document

Pass order:

1. Trigger clarity: define when the SOP should be used.
2. Input clarity: list required files, tools, permissions, or context.
3. Step clarity: make each step actionable.
4. Failure mode pass: add what to do when a step fails.
5. Owner handoff: clarify who decides, approves, or verifies.
6. Completion criteria: define what done means.
7. Final usability pass: produce unresolved questions and handoff notes.

Checker:

- The SOP has a clear trigger.
- Required inputs are listed.
- Steps are actionable and ordered.
- Failure modes are addressed.
- Completion criteria are checkable.
- Human approval points are explicit.

Human gate:

- A process decision needs an owner.
- Access, permissions, or tools are missing.
- A failure mode needs a policy choice.
- The SOP changes team responsibilities.
