# Rule Examples by Type of Work

Worked examples for proposing rule candidates. Use these to calibrate what a good rule looks like for each kind of knowledge work — not to copy verbatim. Every real rule must be built from the user's own files and corrections.

## Content Creators

**linkedin-writing.md** — loads on `linkedin-posts/**/*`
- Open with a bold, scroll-stopping hook: a claim, number, or outcome in the first line. Never open with a rhetorical question.
- When given a reference post, match its structure, length, rhythm, and pacing exactly — copying the topic but not the structure is the usual failure.
- Let the insight dictate the takeaway. Not every post ends with the same kind of conclusion.
- Reference files: the user's post framework doc for voice; their folder of published posts for concrete examples.

**newsletter-drafts.md** — loads on `newsletter-drafts/**/*`
- Include specific examples and case studies from the user's own experience; flag any section that makes a claim without one.
- Free content must feel complete on its own, never like a teaser for the paid tier.
- Reference files: the user's writing guidelines; their best-performing published posts.

## Coaches and Consultants

**client-deliverables.md** — loads on `clients/**/*`
- Always use the client's terminology, never the consultant's internal shorthand.
- Include an executive summary on any deliverable over 2 pages.
- Format recommendations as numbered action items with owners and deadlines.
- Never include internal pricing or margin notes in client-facing documents. The cost of one slip is why this is a hard line.

**proposals.md** — loads on `proposals/**/*`
- Lead with the client's problem, not the consultant's capabilities.
- Include 3 pricing tiers with clear scope differences.
- Reference past similar projects by outcome, not by client name.
- Keep proposals under 5 pages unless the client asked for more detail.

## Entrepreneurs

**brand-voice.md** — loads on `marketing/**/*` (or wherever brand content lives)
- First person plural ("we") for brand content; first person singular ("I") for founder content.
- No superlatives without data behind them. Every claim needs a proof point: a number, a testimonial, or a case study.
- Reference files: the brand guidelines doc.

**campaign-briefs.md** — loads on `campaigns/**/*`
- Every brief includes: objective, audience, key message, CTA, and success metrics. Missing one means the brief is not done.
- Connect every creative option to the campaign objective — options without a rationale get cut.

## Knowledge Workers

**research-reports.md** — loads on `reports/**/*` or `research/**/*`
- Cite sources with links; no unsupported claims.
- Lead every finding with "what this means" before showing the data.
- Flag confidence levels: separate what the data clearly shows from interpretation.

**source-evaluation.md** — loads on `research/**/*`
- Prioritize primary sources and peer-reviewed research over blog posts and opinion pieces.
- Anything older than 12 months gets a recency disclaimer.
- Cross-reference claims across at least 2 independent sources before including them.
- Note when cited content sits behind a paywall.

## The shared pattern

Each rule: targets one type of work (the paths), enforces standards the user would otherwise repeat by hand (the body, traceable to real corrections), and points at the user's real material for depth (the references). If a proposed candidate doesn't fit that pattern, it probably belongs in CLAUDE.md, a context file, or memory instead.
